## What changed

The first pass removes specific causes of dropped input and interrupted motion while preserving strict execution.

- **Run:** immediate stop, block, crouch, jump or attack; down-forward no longer keeps the run alive.
- **Jump flow:** air normals and Kogan's air gun preserve horizontal travel. Hop identity survives an attack, its recovery and Roman Cancel, including the hop hurtbox. A hop adds **0f** landing recovery; a full jump adds **2f**. Uppercuts and other committed moves retain their own landing tax. Touchdown no longer erases air hitstun. A button on safe hop touchdown becomes a ground action; FL does not accidentally fire/spend an air-gun chamber.
- **Strict links:** recovery, block/hitstun, wakeup, feint and backdash accept an input on the first free frame. An early normal is still not buffered through recovery, and normals still cannot chain.
- **Client timing/input:** a fixed clock schedules 60 Hz independently of display rate, clears pause/menu backlog, and provides exactly one requested training step. Button edges and short up-taps survive between simulation ticks; pad button press/release events survive a single render-frame event drain. Facing conversion happens when a tick consumes input. Stage/attack animation and event display timers follow simulation timing.
- **Animation increments:** authored walk and signature-attack cells for Kogan/Raya, selected by preparation/contact/recovery; dedicated wide thrust cells retain Kogan's full saber and cape. Foot anchors stabilize the unequal source sheets. Small source-row gutters prevent adjacent-row fragments. The attacker draws in front so a defender's cape does not cover the attack. Duplicated static ghost trails are removed. Kogan's EX rekka now uses his saber presentation rather than the wave pose. Other moves retain the established pose set with limited phase transitions.
- **Repeatable review:** `--polish-preview` runs both bodies through approach, run, hop, full jump, a complete rekka, knockdown/getup, and a heavy-whiff punish, through real inputs. `--capture` exports 30 fps PNGs and per-tick state hashes. Its own test verifies each rekka reaches three hits and each whiff is actually punished.

No move damage, startup/active/recovery tables, cancel windows or gauge costs were retuned. No camera effects, roster additions, audio or netcode were added. The sim still has zero dependencies and remains integer 60 Hz.

## Combo rule and documentation

The blanket five-hit rejection was removed from current law/QA and crate copies. **Kogan jab → jab xx full rekka** is now proven: **five hits, 170 damage, hard knockdown**. This is permitted by Evan's revised rule; it still needs balance judgment in play. Typical routes remain two or three hits, with spacing/reads/footsies sufficient for effective play.

The old hop-overhead test name and QA trial prose incorrectly reversed standing/crouching defense; names/prose now match the existing correct behavior: hop overhead beats crouch block and loses to stand block.

Generated airtime prose now uses the simulation's actual gravity-before-position order: Kogan **41f full / 25f hop**, Raya **34f full / 20f hop**, plus 4f prejump and the applicable landing tax. This fixes a two-frame documentation error without changing the arcs.

## Limits and next playtest

This is a first flow pass, **not a claim of finished animation or competitive validation**. Four-key signature sequences coexist with older single-pose normals, reactions, air attacks and specials. Style continuity, transition quality, additional in-betweens and full-kit motion need more art passes. Run remains a glide pose; complete cloth motion is still needed. The current renderer draws simulation positions at 60 Hz without interpolating between ticks.

No physical arcade stick was detected on Citadel during this pass. Keyboard/UI checks and headless input tests do not establish stick reliability, hardware latency, or competitive feel. Analog-axis pulses shorter than an event drain still use sampled axis state and need hardware validation. Training replay saves still describe input logs; mid-recording manual resets, swaps or meter edits are not a complete session-state replay contract.

Evan's first stick session should judge:

1. Stop/block/jump/attack out of run, and hop/full-jump landing feel.
2. A tight jab link and a short confirm: do inputs register predictably without becoming easy chains?
3. Heavy whiff recovery, contact weight, and whether the sword/glyph motion teaches timing.
4. Retained pressure: strike/grab/bait, wakeup reversal, and the cost of overcommitting.
5. Kogan's swordwork and Raya's composure in motion. Then choose the next animation/move family to finish.

Balance, landing-frame tuning and the next animation scope should follow that play session before expanding the roster.
